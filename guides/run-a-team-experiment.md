# Run A Team Experiment

This is a two week test for one useful commercial job. It gives a team enough structure to learn something real, without treating people as a productivity experiment.

Use it after a [First Team Session](../workshops/first-team-session.md), or when a few people have already found a prompt or method they think may be worth sharing.

## What You Need

- One approved AI tool
- One safe job to test
- Two to five volunteers
- A fictional example or approved information
- A manager who removes friction and protects the boundary

Use the [Use Case Experiment](../templates/use-case-experiment.md) to record the plan before anyone starts.

## Pick The Right Job

A good first experiment is:

- common enough that people recognise it;
- small enough to test in one sitting;
- safe to practise on;
- easy for a person to check; and
- useful even if the output only becomes a first draft.

It is not a good first experiment if it makes customer commitments, changes systems, handles sensitive information or needs a person to trust the output without checking it.

## Week One: Learn The Shape Of The Work

1. Ask everyone to try the same job once with a fictional or approved sample.
2. Ask them to use a simple before and after comparison: their normal first attempt, then the AI supported attempt.
3. Record what the AI got right, what it got wrong and what input made the difference.
4. Improve the method once. Do not keep changing five things at the same time.

The useful question is not “Was it amazing?” It is “What did it actually help with?”

## Week Two: Repeat Or Stop

Ask volunteers to use the improved method again only when it is safe and appropriate in normal work.

They should record:

- whether they used it again;
- whether the output was easier to check;
- whether it saved effort or improved quality;
- what they still had to fix; and
- whether they would recommend a further test.

No one needs to share customer details or private prompts to provide this learning.

## Review The Evidence Together

Use the [Experiment Review Check](../checks/team-experiment-review.md) and hold a short review. There are four reasonable outcomes:

| Outcome | What it means | What to do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep testing | It helped, but the evidence is still thin | Run a second small test with the same boundary |
| Improve | The job is useful but the input, output or check needs work | Change one thing and try again |
| Share carefully | More than one person found it useful and safe | Turn it into a draft shared asset and name an owner |
| Stop | It added little value or created too much friction | Capture the learning and choose another job |

Stopping a weak method is progress. It frees people to try something better.

## Keep The Numbers Honest

Time saved can be useful to record, but do not claim an ROI from a handful of attempts. Early signals tell you whether a method deserves more testing. They do not prove a team wide productivity figure.

The [Monthly Team Review](../templates/monthly-team-review.md) helps a manager decide what the evidence is strong enough to say.
