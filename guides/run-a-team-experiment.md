# Run A Team Experiment

This is a two week test of one useful commercial job. It gives a team enough structure to learn something real, without treating people as a productivity experiment.

Use it after a [First Team Session](../workshops/first-team-session.md), or when a few people have found a prompt or method they think may be worth sharing.

## What You Need

- One approved AI tool
- One safe job to test
- Two to five volunteers
- A fictional example or approved information
- A manager who clears obstacles and keeps people inside the boundary

Write the plan in the [Use Case Experiment](../templates/use-case-experiment.md) before anyone starts.

## Pick The Right Job

A good first experiment is:

- common enough that people recognise it;
- small enough to test in one sitting;
- safe to practise on;
- easy for a person to check; and
- useful even if the output only becomes a first draft.

It's a poor first experiment if it makes customer commitments, changes systems, handles sensitive information, or needs a person to trust the output without checking it.

## Week One: Learn The Shape Of The Work

1. Ask everyone to try the same job once with a fictional or approved sample.
2. Ask them to compare before and after: their normal first attempt, then the attempt with AI.
3. Record what the AI got right, what it got wrong and what input made the difference.
4. Improve the method once. Don't change five things at the same time.

The useful question isn't "Was it amazing?" It's "What did it actually help with?"

## Week Two: Repeat Or Stop

Ask volunteers to use the improved method again, but only when it's safe and suitable in their normal work.

They should record:

- whether they used it again;
- whether the output was easier to check;
- whether it saved effort or improved quality;
- what they still had to fix; and
- whether they would recommend another test.

No one needs to share customer details or private prompts to report this.

## Review The Evidence Together

Hold a short review using the [Experiment Review Check](../checks/team-experiment-review.md). There are four reasonable outcomes:

| Outcome | What it means | What to do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep testing | It helped, but the evidence is still thin | Run a second small test with the same boundary |
| Improve | The job is useful but the input, output or check needs work | Change one thing and try again |
| Share carefully | More than one person found it useful and safe | Turn it into a draft shared asset and name an owner |
| Stop | It added little value or got in the way too much | Record what you learned and choose another job |

Stopping a weak method is progress. It frees people to try something better.

## Keep The Numbers Honest

It can help to record time saved, but don't claim a return on investment from a handful of attempts. Early results tell you whether a method deserves more testing. They don't prove a productivity figure for the whole team.

The [Monthly Team Review](../templates/monthly-team-review.md) helps a manager decide what the evidence is strong enough to say.
