# Work With One Approved Tool

It is completely normal for a commercial team to have access to only one approved AI tool. You do not need ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini and a stack of integrations to build a useful habit.

The tool matters less than the method: give it safe information, ask it to do a clear job and check what comes back.

## Keep The Setup Simple

Before you use the tool, find out:

1. what the organisation has approved it for;
2. what information must stay out of it;
3. where people can find the current guidance; and
4. who to ask if the boundary is unclear.

Do not guess. A good prompt does not make an unapproved use safe.

## Start With Three Jobs

Choose jobs with a clear beginning and end. These are good first options:

| Job | Safe starting input | Useful output | Human check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting preparation | Public company information and your own approved notes | Questions, facts to verify and an agenda | Confirm facts and decide what matters |
| Follow up draft | Approved, non sensitive call notes or a fictional example | A short draft in your own voice | Check accuracy, promises and tone before sending |
| Internal handover | Approved internal notes | A clear summary of actions, risks and open questions | Confirm the action owner and the next step |

Pick one. A long list of possible uses is not a starting plan.

## Use The Same Four Part Prompt

You can use this structure in almost any approved AI tool.

> **Job:** I need help with [one clear job].
> **Input:** Use only the information below. If something is missing, say so rather than guessing.
> **Output:** Give me [the exact format you need]. Keep it [short, direct, customer friendly, internal].
> **Check:** Flag assumptions, do not invent facts or commitments, and leave the final decision and action to me.

Add the approved information beneath it. Save a copy of what worked in your own notes or in the [Team Learning Log](../templates/team-learning-log.md).

## Make The Tool Fit Your Team

When someone says, "That answer was not very good," do not jump straight to a different model. Ask four simpler questions:

1. Was the job clear enough?
2. Did it have the information it needed?
3. Did we ask for a useful output shape?
4. Did we tell it what to avoid inventing or deciding?

Usually the first improvement is in one of those places.

## Do Not Turn It Into A Tool Competition

You may eventually compare tools. That is not the useful first step for most teams.

Start with one approved tool, one safe job and one way to review the output. If that works, you have a method you can improve. If it does not, you have learned where the real problem is.

## When You Are Ready To Go Further

Run a small [Team Experiment](run-a-team-experiment.md) rather than making a team wide promise. The point is to find what deserves to become a shared asset, not to make everyone use AI in the same way.
