# Call Follow Up Draft

This shared asset helps a salesperson turn approved call notes into a clear first draft follow up. It saves you starting from a blank page. It doesn't send customer messages or decide what was agreed.

## Use This When

- you have notes from a call that are safe to use in your approved AI tool;
- you want a short, customer friendly first draft; and
- you will check and send the final message yourself.

## Do Not Use This When

- the notes include information that shouldn't go into the tool;
- you're not sure what was agreed on the call;
- the email needs a legal, pricing or contractual commitment; or
- you want the tool to send the message or update a system.

## What To Provide

1. The customer name and company, only if the tool is approved for them.
2. The safe call notes.
3. The next step you agreed, if there was one.
4. Any preference on tone or length.

If any of this is missing, leave it out. The method should flag the gap, not fill it in.

## Use It In Three Steps

1. Read the [prompt](prompt.md) and add the information you're allowed to use.
2. Read the draft. Don't send it yet.
3. Run the [quality check](quality-check.md), edit the draft in your own voice and decide what happens next.

## What It Produces

- a clear subject line;
- a short recap of the conversation;
- agreed actions and owners, only where the notes support them;
- one proposed next step, only if it was agreed or is clearly marked as a suggestion; and
- a short list of assumptions or missing details to check.

## What A Person Still Does

The salesperson checks every fact, decides what to say, approves the final email and sends it. The method does none of those things.

## What We Learned Before Sharing It

The [fictional Northstar asset review](example.md) shows the team improving the method after a small test. It's an example of the evidence a team should look for, not proof that the method will work everywhere.

## Keep It Current

**Asset owner:** Add a named team owner before sharing this within a real organisation.
**Review date:** Add a date after the first month of normal use.

If people stop finding it useful, update it or retire it. Don't let it become a forgotten prompt in a folder.
