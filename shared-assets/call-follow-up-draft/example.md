# Northstar Asset Review

> Fictional example. Northstar, its team, customers and activity are invented.

Northstar first tested a follow up drafting method with five volunteers. The first version was useful for structure but sometimes made a possible next step sound agreed. The team did not share it yet.

## The Second Test

Three volunteers used a revised version over two weeks, with their approved tool and approved information. The revised prompt asked the tool to flag unclear details and place any unagreed next step under a clearly labelled suggestion.

| What the volunteers found | What the team changed or kept |
| --- | --- |
| The draft removed the blank page after calls with clear notes | Kept the short, structured email format |
| The assumptions list made people catch missing owners and dates | Kept **Check Before Sending** as a required part of the output |
| One draft still sounded too generic | Added the instruction to edit in the salesperson's own voice |
| No one wanted the method to send messages | Kept sending and final approval with the salesperson |

## Why It Earned A Place

The method was used more than once by more than one person. The safe input, useful output and human check were clear. The team knew its common mistakes and chose an owner to review it after a month.

It became a shared team method, not a mandatory process. People could adapt it when the situation called for a different approach.

## What This Does Not Prove

- That every follow up will be better.
- That the team saved a specific number of hours.
- That the method will work in another organisation without adapting it.
- That the AI should make a customer commitment or send an email.

The useful evidence was simple: several people found it helpful, understood its limits and could check it properly.
