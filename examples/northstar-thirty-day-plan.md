# Northstar Thirty Day Plan

> Fictional example. Northstar and every person in it are invented.

Northstar has a team of account executives and sales managers. The company already gives people access to an approved general AI tool, but most people either do not use it or use it for vague drafting without a shared standard.

## First Ten Days

Pick two safe tasks:

1. turn approved call notes into a first follow up draft;
2. prepare a call card from public information and approved notes.

Use fictional examples first. Capture what input was needed, what the output got wrong and how long the check took.

**What one Day 3 capture actually looked like**, from a volunteer testing the call card task against a fictional prospect, Underwood Dental Group:

> Input used: a public listing mentioning a third location opening this year, plus internal notes that this was a cold outreach with no prior contact.
> What the output got wrong: it wrote "as agreed, we'll present pricing options at our next call," inventing an agreement that had never happened.
> Time to check: under two minutes once the volunteer knew to look for invented agreement, longer the first time before that pattern was named.

That single capture is what later showed up as the recurring problem in the [Northstar Manager Review](northstar-manager-review.md).

## Days Eleven to Twenty

Invite five volunteers to try one task during normal work using approved information. Each person records:

- the task;
- the time taken before and after;
- what they corrected; and
- whether they would use it again.

Sales managers do not treat this as a performance score. It is learning.

## Days Twenty One to Thirty

Review the evidence with the volunteers and Sales Operations:

- Which task was useful enough to keep testing?
- Which mistakes were common?
- What information or checks need to be standard?
- Is a shared team asset genuinely needed?

The fictional plan does not claim a productivity number, a rollout or a purchase decision. It creates enough evidence, real captures like the one above, not just a general impression, to decide whether one should be considered.
